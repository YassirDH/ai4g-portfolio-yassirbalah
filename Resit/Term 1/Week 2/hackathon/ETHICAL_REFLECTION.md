# Ethical reflection: CareCircle

**When it is wrong.** The most dangerous error is a missed emergency. If someone writes "oma kon vanochtend niet opstaan" ("grandma couldn't get up this morning") the AI might call it `attention` instead of `urgent`, and my red-flag list does not contain those words either. I reduced this risk (the AI must choose the higher level when unsure, and code escalates on words like *gevallen* (fallen), *benauwd* (short of breath) or *chest pain* regardless of the AI), but I cannot remove it. The opposite error is also real: too many false alarms and the family starts ignoring the 🚨 messages. Smaller errors happen too. In testing, the AI could not assign a task because a friend's Telegram name did not match the care team, and it sometimes asks a follow-up question that is not needed. That is why the original message is always stored next to the AI summary, so a person can check what was actually said.

**When it is late or down.** A message in a group chat is not an emergency call. If something serious happens, the family must call 112 or the GP first, not post it and wait. If Gemini, n8n or Telegram is down, nothing is logged and no alert is sent. Because CareCircle replies to every care update, a missing reply is a visible signal that the system is not working, but I did not build automatic error alerts, and at night nobody may read the group at all. The weekly report also depends on a trial account that ends; in real use that would need a paid or self-hosted setup.

**Privacy and dignity.** These are health data about a person with dementia, who is not in the group and may not be able to consent. In the demo I only use a fictional case, because on the free Gemini tier Google may use inputs to improve its models and human reviewers may read them. Real use would require a paid tier or a local model, a data agreement, strict access to the spreadsheet, and, above all, talking with the person herself while she can still decide what is shared about her. A care log can easily turn into surveillance.

**Would I trust it?** For coordination, yes: knowing who picks up the prescription and seeing that one person does all the visits is genuinely useful. For safety, only as a backup. CareCircle never decides that something is fine. **Automation stops and a human takes over** at every urgent alert, at every question about medication or symptoms, and at the GP visit: the AI prepares questions, but people ask them and doctors answer them. The care-load signal is a gentle suggestion to talk, never a judgement about who cares enough.
